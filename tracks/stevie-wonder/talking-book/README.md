# Talking Book (1972)

**Artist:** Stevie Wonder  
**Release Year:** 1972  
**Label:** Tamla / Motown  
**Studios:** Electric Lady Studios (NYC), Crystal Sound (Hollywood), Record Plant (LA)  
**Producers:** Stevie Wonder, Malcolm Cecil, Robert Margouleff  

## Overview

*Talking Book* represents the true dawn of Stevie Wonder's "classic period." Having renegotiated his contract with Motown on his 21st birthday, Stevie seized total creative control over his compositions, production, and arrangements. 

## Key Technological Context

- **TONTO (The Original New Timbral Orchestra):** Designed and operated by Malcolm Cecil and Robert Margouleff, TONTO was the world's largest multitimbral modular polyphonic synthesizer. Stevie used it to lay down organic, resonant bass lines that breathed with the pocket.
- **Hohner Clavinet D6 & Mu-Tron III:** On "Superstition", Stevie famously layered Clavinet tracks through envelope filters, creating the percussive, damped rhythm comping that redefined funk keyboard playing.
- **Drums & Pocket:** Stevie tracked drums himself, playing behind the beat on the snare and incorporating syncopated open-hat accents on the offbeats.
